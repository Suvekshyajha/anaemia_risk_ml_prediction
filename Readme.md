Anaemia is quite a common public-health problem among women of reproductive age in Nepal, but its detection typically depends on access to haemoglobin testing, which may be limited in rural settings in Nepal. This study aims to conduct quantitative analysis for predicting anaemia risk from survey-based factors like age, geographical region, etc., to prioritize women for haemoglobin testing.For this, Nepal Demographic and Health Survey data was used particularly focusing on rural women aged 15-49 years who had valid haemoglobin measurements, resulting in 3,371 women.  Anaemia was recorded as either present or absent using WHO-defined thresholds. The model was designed to prioritize women for testing, not replace diagnosis. Several Machine Learning Techniques were used, starting from Basic models like Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, KNN, SVM, and  Ensemble learning techniques like Bagging (Random Forest), Boosting (LightGBM, XGBoost, Adaboost), and stacking, which combines multiple models together for stronger predictions. Models were trained and evaluated using a train–test split. For interpreting model predictions and identifying influential features, SHAP (Shapley Additive Explanations) was used. Among the models, XGBoost worked best i.e AUC 0.70, while the stacking model resulted in the most balanced results (69.3% accuracy). Altitude, geographic region, and age were the strongest predictors of anaemia risk. Decision curve and fairness analysis were conducted for analysing real world usefullness. Decision-curve analysis indicated that this model can help decide which women may be referred for haemoglobin testing. Fairness Analysis showed the model performs with varying efficiency across various provinces so its decision threshold should be Fine-tuned for each region.
